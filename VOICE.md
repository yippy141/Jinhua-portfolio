# Voice

How to write copy for this site. This is the practical companion to
`CONSTITUTION.md` §2; where they overlap, the Constitution wins.

## Rules

- **State what the thing literally does.** Lead with the action, not the ambition.
  "A guided questionnaire that shows which schools of IR theory match your
  assumptions," not "a reflective instrument for interrogating priors."
- **Prefer specific nouns and verbs.** Name the parts: mining, refining, metal,
  magnet. Filter, compare, inspect, track.
- **Explain what the reader can inspect or change.** Point to the sources,
  weights, filters, or sensitivity tests they can open.
- **Distinguish evidence from interpretation.** Separate what is announced from
  what is operating; separate a claim from a guess. Say which is which.
- **Write in the first person** when describing Jinhua's role or motivation.
  "I built this to…", not "this project was built to…".

## Avoid

- These words unless technically necessary: "source-backed", "intelligence
  product", "hard domain", "living atlas", "landscape", "ecosystem", "navigate",
  "leverage", "robust".
- Manifesto language and manufactured aphorisms ("confident presentation, humble
  architecture" is a design principle, not body copy).
- AI filler (see CONSTITUTION.md §2): "genuinely", "delve", "seamless",
  "game-changing", "unlock", "empower", hype adjectives.
- Repeated em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon, or rewrite.

## Shape of a good project description

One or two sentences. First sentence: what it is and what it does. Second
sentence: what the reader can filter, compare, or inspect, and what the data
separates (announcement vs. capacity, claim vs. source).
